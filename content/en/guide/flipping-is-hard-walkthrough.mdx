## The Four Rules That Govern Every Jump

Before you learn a single advanced technique, you need to understand the rules the whole movement system is built on. According to community reports from the [Flipping is Hard Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3826670/discussions/0/804594822448130357/), four properties of the jump determine everything else you'll do in the game.

| Rule | What It Means | How to Exploit It |
|---|---|---|
| Jump cooldown | There's a short delay — roughly a quarter second — between jumps | Plan a rhythm instead of mashing; a buffered second press gets eaten |
| Surface reset | Touching any solid surface restores your jump | Chain wall touches to stack multiple jumps in a row |
| Midair jump | You can spend a jump while airborne | Save it as a recovery or as an extension of a wall launch |
| Air control | You can steer and drift while off the ground | Adjust your landing mid-flight instead of committing to the launch angle |

That third rule is the one players underuse most. A midair jump isn't just a panic button — it's the foundation of nearly every wall jump variant in this Flipping is Hard walkthrough. The fourth rule matters just as much: because you keep steering after takeoff, a slightly sloppy launch can still land cleanly if you correct early.

One more thing worth internalizing: the cooldown applies to *every* jump, including wall jumps. If you tap jump the instant you touch a wall, nothing happens and you slide off. Get in the habit of touching, then jumping a beat later.

## Flipping is Hard Walkthrough: Jump Techniques Compared

Not all jumps are the same. The game reads your body orientation and your input at the moment you press jump, which is why two players pressing the same key get wildly different results. Here's how the core jumps compare.

| Technique | Input | Result | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat high jump (A) | Stay vertical, hold no direction, press jump | Maximum vertical pop from a standstill | Easy |
| Moving high jump | Release forward for about half a second, then jump | Vertical lift while keeping momentum | Medium |
| Tilted high jump (B) | Sit near vertical but tilted roughly 15 degrees, press jump | Higher, flatter launch — ideal wall jump setup | Medium |
| Spring jump | Jump as you contact a spring, or jump at the apex | Extra height on contact, extra distance at the peak | Easy |
| Corner jump | Jump off a corner or edge of your body | Small bonus height and distance | Medium |

The spring jump is the friendliest technique on the list and a good confidence builder. Player experience suggests two distinct timings: jumping immediately after hitting a spring adds height, while jumping at the top of the arc adds distance. Test both on the same spring and you'll feel the difference within a few attempts.

The flat high jump is your reset button. Any time you land somewhere safe and want to gain height without drifting, go vertical and press jump with no direction held. The moving version — releasing forward briefly, then jumping — is the same idea adapted for when you're already rolling.

## Wall Jumps, Explained (A, B, and C)

Wall jumps are where Flipping is Hard stops being a flipping game and starts being a movement game. Community reports describe three distinct variants, each with a different setup and a different payoff.

| Variant | Setup | Practical Payoff | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall jump A | Flat high jump straight into a wall, then jump again | Two to three touches; you'll usually get pushed off after the third | Easy |
| Wall jump B | Tilted high jump into a wall from about a phone's length away | You land flat and higher on the wall, unlocking a third and sometimes fourth jump | Medium–Hard |
| Wall jump C | Launch parallel to a wall, grazing it just enough to touch | Extends horizontal distance; chains into A, B, and long jumps | Very Hard |

For **Wall jump A**, the sequence is straightforward: perform a flat high jump against the wall, then jump again. You may need to tap forward or back first so your body actually makes contact — if there's a gap, the jump won't register as a wall touch. A third touch is possible, but the game tends to push you away, so treat it as a bonus rather than a plan.

**Wall jump B** is the one worth grinding. Approach from roughly a phone's length out, hold yourself near vertical but tilted about 15 degrees, and press jump on contact. Instead of bouncing away, you should end up flat against the wall and higher than you started, which gives you the angle for a third jump. If you get shoved outward, rotate quickly to re-touch the wall and you can often squeeze out a fourth.

**Wall jump C** is the expert tier. You launch almost parallel to the wall so that you only graze it, then jump to convert that touch into extra distance. Chaining it with the other variants is possible but genuinely hard — the kind of thing to attempt after everything else feels automatic.

## Sprint Flipping and Building Real Speed

Speed in Flipping is Hard comes from sprint flipping, and sprint flipping comes from geometry. The rule is simple: jump when your bottom is flat against the ground while holding forward. The execution is where people lose runs.

Community reports recommend rolling onto the thin long edge of the phone before you flip — it makes building speed dramatically easier. From there, the sequence looks like this:

| Step | Action | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Roll onto the long, thin edge of the phone | Reduces friction and sets up a clean rotation |
| 2 | Angle the camera slightly off-axis | Gives you a reference for when your body is flat |
| 3 | Hold forward and straighten the camera | Lines your momentum up with the direction you want |
| 4 | Jump at the moment your bottom is flat | Produces a forward flip rather than a stumble |
| 5 | Release forward for a fraction of a second after takeoff | Prevents over-flipping past your landing |

That fifth step is the fix for the single most common failure in this Flipping is Hard walkthrough: the over-flip. You launch perfectly, keep holding forward, rotate one half-turn too far, and land on your back. Letting go of forward briefly after takeoff keeps the rotation under control.

If you're struggling to find the launch window, tap forward while flat rather than holding it. That helps you locate the sweet spot between flipping and rolling — the narrow moment where a jump turns into speed instead of a tumble.

## Advanced Tech: Corner Jumps, Micro-Movement, and Practice

### Corner jumps

You can squeeze a little extra height and distance out of a jump by launching off your corners rather than a flat face. The gain is small, but in a game built on inches it's the difference between grabbing a ledge and restarting. Try it on a familiar jump first — pick a gap you can already clear and see how much margin the corner launch gives you.

### Micro-movement

A community-reported trick worth knowing: if you press flat against a cliff and vibrate, you can hold a 90-degree angle, then wiggle the camera to make tiny positional adjustments. It's slow, it's awkward, and it's exactly what you want when a jump needs two pixels of extra alignment.

### A practice plan that sticks

| Drill | Goal | Suggested Time |
|---|---|---|
| Flat high jumps on open ground | Feel the cooldown and the no-input launch | 5 minutes |
| Spring jumps, both timings | Learn height timing vs. distance timing | 5 minutes |
| Wall jump A on a single wall | Land two clean touches in a row | 10 minutes |
| Wall jump B from one phone-length out | Convert a tilted launch into a flat wall stick | 15 minutes |
| Sprint flipping on a long straight | Hold a speed line for five consecutive flips | 10 minutes |
| Corner jumps on a known gap | Add measurable margin to a jump you've already cleared | 5 minutes |

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Mashing jump | Inputs vanish, character feels unresponsive | Wait out the cooldown, then press once |
| Jumping on wall contact | No wall jump, immediate slide | Touch first, jump a beat later |
| Holding forward through the flip | Over-rotation and back landings | Release forward briefly after takeoff |
| Ignoring air control | Missing landings you should have made | Steer during flight, not just at launch |
| Practicing everything at once | Slow, shapeless progress | Drill one technique per session |

## FAQ

**Is there a Flipping is Hard walkthrough for the hardest challenge?**
Community reports suggest a specific route for the toughest jump: use a high jump combined with a wall jump from the middle-bottom of the top net to reach the wooden board. Practice wall jump B until the flat wall stick is reliable before attempting it.

**Why does my jump sometimes do nothing?**
Almost always the cooldown. There's a short delay between jumps, so a press that lands too soon after the previous one is ignored. Slow your rhythm down before you assume the game is broken.

**How do I stop over-flipping when sprinting?**
Release forward for a fraction of a second right after takeoff. You keep the momentum but stop adding rotation, which keeps your landing flat.

**Does touching a wall really reset my jump?**
Yes — any solid surface restores your jump, which is why wall jump chains work at all. The limit isn't the reset, it's the pushback that follows each contact.
