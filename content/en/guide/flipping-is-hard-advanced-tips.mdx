## The Four Rules Every Advanced Run Is Built On

Every technique below sits on top of four mechanics the game never spells out. Community reports describe them plainly, and once you internalize them, the rest of these Flipping is Hard advanced tips stop feeling like tricks and start feeling like physics.

| Rule | What it actually means | How to exploit it |
|---|---|---|
| Short jump cooldown | There is a delay of roughly a quarter second between jumps | Stop mashing; press once, deliberately, then wait |
| Surface contact resets jump | Touching any solid object restores your jump | Treat walls, cliffs, and ledges as ammo, not obstacles |
| Jumping works in midair | You are not limited to one jump per landing | Fire a second jump before you touch down to extend arcs |
| Air control exists | You can steer while airborne | Correct mid-flight instead of re-approaching the jump |

The cooldown is the one that catches most players. Because the gap is short but real, panicked inputs get eaten and you lose the height you were counting on. If a jump feels like it "didn't register," you almost certainly pressed during the cooldown.

Surface resets are the reason wall jumps exist at all. Every wall you graze is a fresh jump, which is why the fastest routes in Flipping is Hard hug geometry instead of avoiding it. Air control then lets you convert those resets into direction changes mid-flight.

## High Jumps, Spring Jumps, and Air Control

The high jump is the backbone of the whole system. According to community reports, the cleanest version happens when the phone is perfectly vertical and you are holding no directional input — just jump. The result is a straight vertical pop that sets up wall contact.

The moving version is a small rhythm trick: release forward for about half a second, then hit jump. You keep your momentum but get the vertical lift instead of a forward tumble.

Springs are the other vertical tool, and timing changes what you get out of them.

| Jump type | Setup | What you get | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| High jump (stationary) | Vertical phone, no inputs, jump | Maximum height | Reaching ledges, setting up wall jumps |
| High jump (moving) | Release forward ~0.5s, then jump | Height while keeping speed | Chaining height into a sprint line |
| Spring jump (early) | Jump as the spring contacts you | Extra height | Climbing to a higher platform |
| Spring jump (late) | Jump at the peak of the spring launch | Extra distance | Clearing long gaps |
| Corner jump | Jump off a corner edge | Small bonus height and distance | Squeezing out the last few inches |

That last row is the most underrated. Corner jumps only give you a sliver of extra height and distance, but a sliver is often the difference between a clean landing and a restart.

## Wall Jumps Ranked: A, B, and C

Wall jumps are where Flipping is Hard advanced tips separate from casual play. The community guide breaks them into three variants, and each one has a different risk-to-reward profile.

| Variant | Setup | Difficulty | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall jump A | High jump against a wall, then jump again; tap forward or back if needed | Low | Two, sometimes three wall contacts |
| Wall jump B | Approach from about a phone-length away, near vertical, tilted roughly 15 degrees, then jump | Medium | Higher contact on the wall, enabling a third and even a fourth jump |
| Wall jump C | Launch parallel to a wall so you just barely touch it, then jump | High | Distance extension and chaining into A, B, and long jumps |

**Wall jump A** is your bread and butter. Get vertical against the wall, jump, and if the game doesn't register the contact, tap forward or back once to nudge yourself into the surface. You can usually get a second and sometimes a third touch before the phone pushes away.

**Wall jump B** is the height extender. Coming in at a slight tilt rather than perfectly flat means you contact the wall higher up and stay glued to it long enough for another jump. From there, a quick rotation back into the wall can buy you a fourth.

**Wall jump C** is the show-off option. Skim a wall at speed, tap jump, and carry the distance. Chaining it is genuinely difficult — the community guide admits as much — so treat it as a route optimization tool, not a survival tool.

## Sprint Flipping and Corner Jumps

Sprint flipping is how you build speed. The sequence is short, but the timing window is unforgiving.

| Step | Input | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land with the bottom of the phone flat to the ground | Landing on an edge kills the setup |
| 2 | Roll onto the long thin side first (optional but recommended) | Skipping this makes speed harder to build |
| 3 | Hold forward | Tapping forward instead of holding it |
| 4 | Jump | Jumping before the phone is flat |
| 5 | Release forward for a beat if you start to over-rotate | Holding forward through the whole flip |

Over-flipping is the classic failure. The phone rotates past the landing and you lose the run, so letting go of forward for a fraction of a second keeps the rotation honest.

Camera angle matters more than it should. Player experience suggests angling the camera slightly, holding forward, then straightening the camera and jumping — and tapping forward while flat to find the sweet spot between a flip and a roll.

There's also a stability trick worth knowing: if you go flat against a cliff and vibrate, you can hold a near-90-degree angle and use small camera wiggles for micro-adjustments. It's fiddly, but it's the difference between sliding off a ledge and setting up a jump.

## Practice Drills That Actually Build Speed

Reading Flipping is Hard advanced tips only gets you so far. Split a session into focused blocks so each mechanic gets real repetitions instead of vague "practice the level" time.

| Drill | What you're training | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|
| Flat-ground flip ladder | Sprint flip timing and the forward-release habit | 5 minutes |
| Wall A ladder | Consistent surface resets on a single wall | 5 minutes |
| Angled wall approach | Wall jump B height and the follow-up rotation | 5 minutes |
| Spring timing swaps | Deliberately alternating early and late spring jumps | 5 minutes |
| Corner hop reps | Finding the corner edge sweet spot | 5 minutes |
| Full speed run | Chaining everything under pressure | 10 minutes |

The order matters. Do the drills fresh and the speed run tired — that mirrors how a real attempt feels, where the early sections are clean and the last stretch is where technique falls apart.

## FAQ

**How long is the jump delay in Flipping is Hard?**
Community reports put it at roughly a quarter of a second. That's short enough to feel instant in normal play but long enough to swallow a panicked second press, which is why deliberate inputs beat mashing on every hard section.

**How do you get more than two wall jumps in a row?**
Use wall jump A to establish contact, then switch to the angled approach from wall jump B. Coming in at a slight tilt keeps you close to the wall and higher up, which is what allows a third and sometimes fourth jump before the phone drifts away.

**Why do I over-flip when sprint flipping?**
Almost always because forward is still held through the rotation. Release forward for a beat during the initial flip, and roll onto the long thin side of the phone before jumping to make speed building far more consistent.

**What's the fastest way to improve using these Flipping is Hard advanced tips?**
Pick one mechanic per session. Spend five minutes on flat-ground sprint flips, five on wall contact, and five on spring timing, then finish with a full run. Isolating inputs builds muscle memory much faster than replaying a whole level and hoping the timing clicks.

The through-line in all of it is patience with a very twitchy phone. The mechanics are simple — a cooldown, a surface reset, an air jump, and air control — and every advanced route in the game is just those four rules stacked in a clever order.
