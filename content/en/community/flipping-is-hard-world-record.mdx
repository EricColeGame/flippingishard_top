## How to Make Your Own Flipping is Hard World Record Attempt Count

Most failed record attempts do not fail because of skill. They fail because of process — bad timing setup, missing footage, or a run submitted to the wrong board. Work through these steps in order and you will avoid the avoidable mistakes.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the category rules before your first attempt | A run that breaks a rule is worthless no matter how fast it is |
| 2 | Choose one board and stay on it | Splitting practice across categories slows progress on all of them |
| 3 | Set up a timer that records in-game time | The leaderboard ranks on IGT, and milliseconds decide ties |
| 4 | Record every attempt with video | Verification requires proof, and a lost PB is a painful loss |
| 5 | Use the community practice trainer for hard sections | Segment practice beats full-run grinding for closing big gaps |
| 6 | Submit through the official board with your footage | Keeps your run in the verification queue instead of a forum post |

A few additional tips from player experience:

- **Grind segments, not full runs.** If your best pace dies in one specific section, replay that section fifty times instead of restarting the whole game fifty times.
- **Watch the leader's footage frame by frame.** With only two verified runs at the top of the Hard board, there is not much data — which makes every second of the record holder's run valuable.
- **Track your splits.** A run that feels slow but finishes fast is more useful than a run that feels fast and dies at the end.
- **Respect the reset threshold.** Decide in advance how far behind pace you are willing to fall before restarting, or you will burn hours on dead runs.
