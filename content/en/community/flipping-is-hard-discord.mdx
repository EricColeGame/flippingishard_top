## Etiquette: How to Be Useful in the Flipping is Hard Discord

Small servers live or die on tone. Because the developers are active and reachable, the community has stayed unusually friendly — but that only holds if new members don't treat it like a customer support queue.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Search before asking about a known skip or bug | Paste the same question into four channels |
| Post a clip when you report a bug | Describe a bug with no context and no level name |
| Tag spoilers for late-game areas and story beats | Spoil the narrator's jokes or voicemail story for new players |
| Say which mode you're playing (Chill vs. standard) | Assume everyone is running the same difficulty |
| Thank people who answer, even if the answer is "skill issue" | Argue with moderators about channel rules |

A few practical tips that will make you popular fast:

- **Use Chill Mode as your qualifier.** Player experience suggests Chill Mode with checkpoints takes roughly 3–6 hours and teaches the rotation physics without the punishment. Ask questions after you've cleared a few areas.
- **Name the level.** "Storm Drain, third ceiling gap" gets a real answer; "I'm stuck" gets a shrug.
- **Share your splits, not just your time.** Runners care about where you lost seconds, not your personal best.
- **Bring clips to bug reports.** The developers have shipped fixes based on footage, including collision tweaks that stopped Flippy getting snagged on ceilings.

## Using the Community to Actually Get Better

The skill curve here is closer to a physics game than a platformer. Community reports compare it to car control in Rocket League: simple inputs, enormous room for creativity. That means the fastest way to improve is to watch people who've already broken the movement down.

| Your Stage | Best Question to Ask | What You'll Get Back |
|---|---|---|
| First hour | "How do I stop over-rotating on landings?" | Short clips showing micro-adjustments and vibration control |
| Mid-climb | "What's the intended route through this area?" | Route descriptions plus a note on which shortcuts are risky |
| Post-clear | "Where are the 16 NPC challenges?" | Locations, difficulty warnings, and cosmetic rewards |
| Speedrunning | "Which skips survived the latest patch?" | Updated routes, since level design gets adjusted between builds |

Patience matters here. Patch notes have included dozens of minor level design changes at once, which means a route that was optimal last month might be dead today. The Discord is where that knowledge gets corrected in near real time — often faster than any written guide.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Flipping is Hard Discord?**
Yes. The developers point players toward Discord in their Steam announcements, including the update that retired the demo. Always pull the invite from an official post on the Steam hub rather than a third-party listing.

**Do I need to finish the game before joining the Flipping is Hard Discord?**
Not at all. Newcomers are common, and most regulars will simply tell you to start in Chill Mode and come back with questions once the rotation controls click. Just tag spoilers if you push ahead of the group.

**Can I find people to play co-op with?**
Yes. Online co-op supports up to 16 players, includes proximity chat, and revolves around team tools like Beams and Microwaves. Group-up channels fill fastest right after a patch or a content push.

**Where do speedrunners share routes and splits?**
Inside community spaces rather than in-game. The game is built to be speedrun friendly with quick restarts and built-in splits, so runners trade route changes constantly — especially after updates that tweak level geometry.

**Is the game worth it if I only play solo?**
Player experience suggests yes. A solo run through Chill Mode runs a few hours, and completionists report squeezing 20–50 hours out of challenges, cosmetics, and clean runs. The Discord just makes those hours more fun to talk about.
