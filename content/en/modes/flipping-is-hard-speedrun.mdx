## Hard vs. Chill: Choosing Your Flipping Is Hard Speedrun Mode

Every category on the board runs under one of two modes: Hard and Chill. This is the single most important choice a new runner makes, and it's the one people get wrong most often — usually by jumping straight into Hard because it sounds like the "real" version.

| Mode | Difficulty | Typical Run Length | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard | Higher | Shorter top times, more variance | Runners chasing leaderboard position |
| Chill | Lower | Longer, more forgiving | Learning routes and building consistency |

The current Solo Any% Hard board shows a first-place time of 29 m 19 s 224 ms and a second-place time of 45 m 30 s 808 ms. On the Chill side, recent submissions include a first-place run at 51 m 51 s 077 ms and a second-place run at 2 h 42 m 06 s 160 ms. That spread is revealing. In Hard, the gap between first and second is about sixteen minutes. In Chill, the gap between first and second is nearly two hours.

That doesn't mean Chill is easier to win — it means Chill runs are longer and far more variable, so a single mistake costs more wall-clock time. Hard runs are shorter, which compresses the field and makes route precision matter more.

A sensible approach for most new runners: learn the route in Chill, where mistakes are survivable and you can absorb the game's pacing, then move to Hard once you can finish consistently. Player experience in the forums echoes this — consistency first, speed second.

## How to Start Your First Flipping Is Hard Speedrun

You don't need a capture card, a second monitor, or a decade of platforming experience. You need a category, a timer, and enough patience to finish a run that isn't good.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the category rules on the leaderboard | Prevents an automatic rejection |
| 2 | Pick Solo Any% in Chill mode | Shortest path to a completed run |
| 3 | Learn the route in segments | Chunked practice beats full attempts |
| 4 | Practice the rough spots in isolation | Fixes the biggest time losses first |
| 5 | Record with IGT visible | Required for verification on most boards |
| 6 | Submit and wait for moderator review | Don't re-submit if it's pending |

A few practical notes that come up repeatedly in community discussion:

- **Finish bad runs.** A slow completed run teaches you more about pacing than ten restarts.
- **Use a practice trainer if one exists.** The forums reference a community-made trainer for the game, which is exactly the kind of tool that accelerates route learning.
- **Watch the top runs.** The gap between first and second place on Hard suggests the leader is doing something specific. Study it rather than guessing.
- **Don't compare yourself to the leaderboard leader in week one.** Compare yourself to your own last run.
