## Gameplay at a Glance: What Ships in the Launch Build

The premise is deliberately absurd. Flippy is a phone with no legs; little Jeff slipped on a strategically placed banana peel, and Flippy was launched into a storm drain. The entire campaign is a climb back to Jeff across ten distinct areas.

| Feature | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Movement | Rotation only, plus a vibration jump |
| Structure | 10 areas, one vertical goal |
| Co-op | Online, up to 16 players |
| Support tools | Beams and microwaves to help friends |
| Voice | Proximity chat |
| Accessibility | Optional Chill Mode with checkpoints |
| Speedrunning | Quick restarts and built-in splits |
| Side content | 16 NPC challenges, phone customization, unlockable classic mobile games |

The control scheme is the whole joke and the whole challenge. With no legs, you cannot walk — you rotate your body and use a short vibration hop to gain height. A small mistake sends you tumbling back down, which is why the store description warns that falling is part of the design rather than a bug.

Co-op is where the game diverges from a standard rage platformer. Sixteen players share a server, place beams and microwaves to create routes for each other, and talk through proximity chat — meaning the people closest to you in the level are the ones you can hear panicking. For solo players, Chill Mode enables checkpoints and turns a punishing climb into a manageable story run.

Speedrun support is built in rather than bolted on: instant restarts and integrated splits mean you do not need external timers to practice a route. Sixteen NPC side challenges reward players who can execute precise movement, and the phone customization system (cases, charms, stickers) plus unlockable classic mobile games add collectible layers beyond the main climb.
