## What Is Flipping is Hard on Steam?

Flipping is Hard is a physics-driven climbing platformer from indie developer Elegant Horse Studios, sold on Steam as a PC download. You control Flippy, a smartphone with no legs, no arms, and no patience for gravity. The setup is deliberately silly: little Jeff slipped on a strategically placed banana peel, accidentally launched Flippy into a storm drain, and now the two are separated. Your job is to climb back to Jeff for the happy ending the pair deserves.

The twist is the control scheme. There is no walking and no running — your only inputs are rotation and a vibration jump. That sounds simple until you realize every surface is a puzzle and every mistake has a cost measured in altitude lost. The store page says it plainly: this is a difficult game that tests your patience, and falling is part of the challenge.

## Solo or Co-Op: Two Very Different Climbs

Flipping is Hard supports two distinct ways to play, and they feel like different games. Solo is the purist route: you learn the movement, you eat the falls, and you experience the story on your own terms. Co-op turns the same climb into a group project where up to 16 friends can share one very tall, very slippery problem.

The co-op layer is not just "more players on the same ledge." Teams get shared tools — Beams and Microwaves — that let players help each other past tough sections rather than abandoning someone at the bottom of a drop. Proximity chat is built in, which means the yelling is part of the design. Community reports describe co-op sessions as equal parts teamwork and comedy, which tracks with a game that openly lists "friendships" among the things it will test.

| Feature | Solo play | Online co-op (up to 16) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story experience | Full | Full |
| Core climbing challenge | Unchanged | Unchanged, plus crowd chaos |
| Shared tools | Not applicable | Beams and Microwaves |
| Voice communication | None needed | Built-in proximity chat |
| Best for | Learning the movement cleanly | Groups who enjoy failing loudly |
| Frustration level | Personal | Shared, amplified, funnier |

If you are new to the game, a short solo session first is the smart move. Learning the rotation-and-jump rhythm with 15 other people shouting is a memorable way to play, but it is a terrible way to learn.

## Movement, Levels, and the Tools You Share

Everything in Flipping is Hard flows from one idea: momentum is earned, not given. Because Flippy can only rotate and hop, clearing a gap means building the right angle, timing the jump, and accepting that the physics will not forgive a rushed input. Ten areas stand between you and the top, each one layering new surfaces and hazards onto the same tiny moveset.

The game also goes out of its way to accommodate different tolerances for pain. Chill Mode adds checkpoints for players who want the story with less suffering, while the speedrun side of the game offers quick restarts and built-in splits for players who want to measure every wasted frame.

| Mechanic | How it works | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rotate-only movement | Rotation is your only steering | Every angle is a decision |
| Vibration jump | A short hop, not a leap | Precision beats distance |
| Hard climb | 10 areas, one goal at the top | Small mistakes cause big drops |
| Chill Mode | Optional checkpoints | Accessibility without changing the base game |
| Speedrun tools | Quick restarts and built-in splits | Low friction for repeat attempts |
| NPC challenges | 16 characters with side quests | Extra tests for movement specialists |
| Cosmetics | Phone cases, charms, stickers | Personalize Flippy while you suffer |
| In-game phone games | Unlockable classic mobile games | A genuine distraction inside a phone |

That last item deserves a mention: hunting down and unlocking playable retro-style mobile games inside a game about a phone is a joke that lands, and it gives completionists a reason to explore beyond the main climb.

A few practical tips go a long way toward keeping that rating earned in your own library:

- **Aim before you leap.** Rotation sets your launch angle, so line up the tilt first and jump second. Panic-jumping is how most drops happen.
- **Learn one area at a time.** Ten zones is not a lot, but each one adds a wrinkle. Do not rush the climb.
- **Use Chill Mode without shame.** Checkpoints exist so the story stays reachable. You can always replay without them later.
- **Play the opening solo.** Fifteen minutes alone will teach you more than an hour of co-op chaos.
- **Bring the right friends.** Proximity chat is a feature, not a bug. Pick people who laugh at falling.
- **Talk to the NPCs.** Sixteen of them have challenges, and those side quests are the fastest way to sharpen your movement.
- **Check the bundles during sales.** The two-game bundle often costs nearly the same as the standalone game at a discount.

Community reports suggest the difficulty curve is steep but fair — the kind of game where a bad run is usually traceable to one specific mistake rather than random nonsense. That is the hallmark of a well-tuned masocore platformer.

**Is Flipping is Hard worth buying on Steam?**
For fans of difficult physics platformers, yes. The Flipping is Hard Steam version costs $14.99 at full price, runs on modest hardware, and offers both a solo story climb and 16-player co-op. If you dislike games that punish mistakes, try Chill Mode or wait for a deeper sale.

Yes. Online co-op supports up to 16 players, with shared tools like Beams and Microwaves plus built-in proximity chat. It is designed for groups who enjoy laughing at collective failure.

**Does Flipping is Hard have an easy mode?**
It has Chill Mode, an optional setting that enables checkpoints for a more forgiving climb. The base difficulty is unchanged when Chill Mode is off.
