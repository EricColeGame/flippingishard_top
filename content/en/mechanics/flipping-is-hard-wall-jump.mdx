## How Wall Jumps Fit Into Flipping is Hard Movement

Before you touch a wall, you need the four rules that govern every jump you make. Community reports describe a short cooldown of roughly a quarter of a second between jumps, contact with any solid surface resetting your jump, the ability to jump while airborne, and full air control while your character is off the ground.

Those rules are the reason wall jumping exists at all. If touching a surface did not refresh your jump, a wall would be a dead end instead of a launchpad. If you had no air control, you could never rotate back into the wall for a second or third contact. And if there were no cooldown, you could spam jumps and skip the timing puzzle entirely.

| Core rule | What it means in practice | Why it matters for wall jumps |
|---|---|---|
| Jump cooldown | A short delay (player reports ~0.25s) between jumps | Forces deliberate timing instead of mashing |
| Surface reset | Touching any solid surface restores your jump | Every wall contact is a fresh launch |
| Air jump | You can jump while off the ground | Enables multi-contact climbing |
| Air control | You steer while airborne | Lets you rotate back into the wall |
| Spring boost | Jumping off a spring adds height or distance | Sets up aerial wall approaches |
| Corner launch | Jumping off a corner adds extra height and reach | Squeezes out the last few inches on hard routes |

Once those click, the Flipping is Hard wall jump stops feeling like a glitch and starts feeling like geometry.

## The Three Wall Jumps in Flipping is Hard, Explained

The community guide splits wall jumping into three named techniques. They are not interchangeable — each one solves a different problem, and each has a different failure mode.

### Wall Jump A — the vertical starter

Start with a high jump performed while your character is fully vertical and holding no directional input. While already moving, you can pull off the same thing by releasing forward for about half a second and then jumping. Carry that vertical launch into a wall and jump again on contact.

You may need a quick tap of forward or backward first to actually touch the surface. If you are close enough, that second contact lets you jump a third time — though by then you are usually being pushed away from the wall, so the chain ends there.

### Wall Jump B — the angle setup

This is the version that gains real height. Approach the wall from roughly a phone's length away, stay vertical but tilt about 15 degrees, and jump. Done correctly, you should end up flat against the wall and higher up than Wall Jump A leaves you, which opens the door to a third jump. You will drift slightly away after that, so rotate quickly to re-touch the wall and you can squeeze in a fourth.

### Wall Jump C — the parallel skim

Launch yourself almost parallel to a wall so you just barely graze it, then jump to extend your horizontal distance. It is the least forgiving of the three, and while it can be chained into Wall Jump A, Wall Jump B, or a long jump, community players describe those chains as genuinely brutal.

| Variant | Approach | Height gained | Difficulty | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall Jump A | Vertical high jump straight into wall | Low to moderate | Beginner | Learning contact timing, short climbs |
| Wall Jump B | ~15° tilt from a phone's length away | High | Intermediate | Vertical routes, multi-jump chains |
| Wall Jump C | Near-parallel graze | Minimal | Advanced | Crossing gaps along a wall face |

## Step-by-Step: Executing the Flipping is Hard Wall Jump

Timing is everything, so here is the sequence laid out as inputs rather than descriptions. Practice each row until it feels automatic before adding the next.

| Step | Wall Jump A | Wall Jump B |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jump while fully vertical, no directional input | Move to about one phone length from the wall |
| 2 | Stay vertical through the launch | Tilt to roughly 15 degrees, keep the body upright |
| 3 | Drift into the wall | Hit jump on approach |
| 4 | Tap forward or back to make contact if needed | Land flat and high on the wall |
| 5 | Jump immediately on contact | Jump for a third launch |
| 6 | Optional third jump if still touching | Rotate back fast for a fourth contact |

A few details separate a clean rep from a messy one. Keep your camera at a slight angle during the approach, then straighten it before the jump so your momentum carries where you expect. Tapping forward while flat helps you find the sweet spot between flipping and rolling — that threshold is where controlled movement begins.

## Chaining Wall Jumps for Height and Distance

Single wall jumps get you up a ledge. Chains get you across a map. The trick is understanding what each contact gives you and when to stop pushing.

| Chain | Sequence | Result | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring climb | Spring → jump at apex → Wall Jump B | Extra height plus wall contact | Intermediate |
| Double contact | Wall Jump A → immediate re-touch → jump | Two quick launches up one face | Beginner |
| Long ascent | Wall Jump B → third jump → rotate → fourth jump | Maximum vertical gain on a single wall | Advanced |
| Distance extender | Wall Jump C → jump → long jump | Extra horizontal reach | Advanced |
| Mixed chain | Wall Jump C → Wall Jump A or B → long jump | Route-dependent shortcuts | Expert |

One community reply adds a trick worth knowing: if you go flat against a cliff and vibrate in place, you can hold a 90-degree angle, then wiggle the camera for micro-adjustments. That is how players line up the tiny contact needed for a fourth jump when the wall is barely in reach.

Players also report using a high jump into a wall jump from the lower middle of a net to reach a wooden board in the game's hardest challenge — a good example of how the Flipping is Hard wall jump turns a seemingly impossible gap into a two-input solution.

## Common Flipping is Hard Wall Jump Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most failed wall jumps are not timing failures. They are approach failures that happen a second earlier.

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Over-flipping | You rotate past the wall and miss contact | Release forward for half a second mid-flip |
| Jumping during cooldown | Input registers but nothing happens | Wait out the ~0.25s delay before the next press |
| Flat approach | You hit the wall but slide instead of sticking | Add a 15-degree tilt before jumping |
| Camera drift | You land somewhere other than planned | Angle the camera, then straighten it before the jump |
| Panic rotation | You push off after the third contact | Rotate back immediately, not after the drift starts |
| Sprint-flip overshoot | You gain speed but lose the wall entirely | Roll onto the thin long side of the phone before flipping |

Sprint flipping deserves its own note. Jump when your bottom is flat with the ground while holding forward, and let go of forward briefly on the initial flip so you do not over-rotate. Rolling onto the phone's thin long edge first makes building speed dramatically easier — and speed is what makes a Wall Jump C skim possible.

## Practice Drills That Build Reliable Wall Jumps

You cannot learn the Flipping is Hard wall jump by reading about it. Work through these drills in order and do not advance until the previous one is boring.

| Drill | Setup | Goal | Reps before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat sprint flips | Open ground, hold forward, jump | Consistent forward flips without over-rotating | Until you never over-flip |
| Wall Jump A reps | One tall wall, vertical approach | Clean second contact every time | 10 clean reps in a row |
| Spring timing | Any spring near a wall | Jump at apex for height, at climax for distance | 5 of each |
| Two-jump chain | Wall with a ledge above | Wall Jump A into an immediate re-touch | 5 clean chains |
| Full ascent | Wall Jump B route | Third and fourth contacts without falling | 3 full climbs |

Between drills, spend a minute on corner jumps. Launching off a corner gives you a small bump in both height and distance, and that bump is often the difference between landing on a platform and bouncing off its edge.

## FAQ

**What is the easiest way to learn the Flipping is Hard wall jump?**
Start with Wall Jump A. Jump while fully vertical with no directional input, drift into a wall, and jump again on contact. You may need a quick tap of forward or backward to actually touch the surface. Once that feels natural, move to the 15-degree angled approach used in Wall Jump B.

**Why does my wall jump keep pushing me away from the wall?**
That is normal after the third contact in a Wall Jump A chain. The fix is to rotate back toward the wall immediately rather than waiting for the drift to stop. Quick re-touching is what enables a fourth jump in Wall Jump B routes.

**Can I combine a wall jump with a sprint flip?**
Yes, and it is the fastest way to cross a map. Build speed with a sprint flip on flat ground — rolling onto the phone's thin long side first makes this much easier — then convert that momentum into a wall contact. Just remember the short jump cooldown before you press again.

**Is the Flipping is Hard wall jump required for the hardest challenge?**
Community players report yes. One recommended route uses a high jump into a wall jump from the middle bottom of the top net to reach the wooden board, which is a strong argument for learning Wall Jump B before attempting the toughest content.

Get the timing down on one wall, then one route, then one challenge. The Flipping is Hard wall jump rewards patience far more than raw reflexes — and once it clicks, you will wonder how you ever played without it.
