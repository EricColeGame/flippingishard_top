## Jump System Secrets: High Jumps, Spring Jumps, and Air Timing

One of the most useful Flipping is Hard secrets is knowing which jump to use instead of defaulting to the same input every time. A spring jump is exactly what it sounds like: hit a spring, then jump. Jump right after contact for extra height, or jump near the climax of the spring launch for extra distance. The timing changes the payoff.

High jump A is the clean vertical option. Jump while upright and holding no direction, and you get a straight-up launch that is ideal before a wall jump. While moving, you can still high jump by releasing forward for about half a second and then pressing jump. That brief release is the difference between a controlled launch and an over-flip.

| Jump type | Setup | Best use | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring jump | Hit a spring, then jump immediately or at the climax | Gaps that need extra height or distance | Mistiming the spring window |
| Standing high jump | Jump while vertical with no direction held | Starting wall-jump chains | No forward momentum |
| Moving high jump | Release forward briefly, then jump | Turning speed into height | Over-flipping if you hold forward |
| Corner jump | Jump from a corner edge | Squeezing out extra height and distance | Very small sweet spot |

Community reports suggest that spring timing is generous enough to practice but strict enough to punish panic. If you are already moving, the moving high jump is usually the better choice because it converts forward speed into vertical clearance without forcing a full stop. Practice the release-then-jump rhythm until it feels boring; that is when it becomes reliable.

## Wall Jump Secrets: Three Routes Up the Same Wall

Wall jumps are where Flipping is Hard secrets start to feel like actual secrets. The community guide describes three routes, and each one solves a different problem. Wall jump A is the beginner route: perform a high jump against a wall, then jump again. You may need to tap forward or backward first to make clean contact. A third jump is sometimes possible, but you will likely be pushed away from the wall.

Wall jump B is the higher, flatter route. Approach a wall from roughly one phone-length away, stay nearly vertical with a slight tilt, and jump as you meet the wall. The goal is to arrive flat and higher on the wall so you can jump a third time. If you are pushed away, rotate quickly to touch the wall again and extend the chain.

Wall jump C is the distance extender. Launch parallel to a wall just enough to touch it, then jump to carry your momentum farther. It can be chained with wall jump A, wall jump B, and long jumps, but the timing is genuinely difficult.

| Wall-jump route | Setup | Difficulty | Best payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall jump A | High jump into a wall, then jump again | Low to medium | Reliable vertical climbing |
| Wall jump B | Approach from about one phone-length, stay nearly vertical, jump on contact | Medium to high | Higher wall position and extra jumps |
| Wall jump C | Launch parallel to a wall and touch lightly | High | Extra distance and chain potential |

Start with wall jump A until the timing is automatic. Then practice B on a wall with plenty of room, because the quick rotate-and-touch recovery is the part most players miss. One community reply also describes a niche movement trick: if you are flat against a cliff and vibrate, you can hold a 90-degree angle and wiggle the camera for micro-movements. That is player experience, not a required technique, but it shows how much control the physics allows.

## Sprint Flipping and Corner Jumps: Building Speed Without Over-Flipping

Another of the Flipping is Hard secrets is that speed comes from clean launches, not from holding forward forever. To sprint flip, jump when your bottom is flat with the ground while holding forward. During the initial flip, release forward for about half a second so you do not over-flip and miss the landing. Rolling onto the phone's thin long side before flipping makes building speed much easier.

The camera matters too. A community-tested approach is to tap forward while flat to find the sweet spot between flipping and rolling. Put the camera at a slight angle, hold forward, move the camera straight, then jump. That sequence gives you a repeatable launch instead of a frantic one.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Over-flipping | Holding forward through the whole launch | Release forward briefly during the initial flip |
| Losing speed | Jumping from a bad angle | Roll onto the thin long side first |
| Missing the landing | Camera drift mid-jump | Straighten the camera before jumping |
| Inconsistent launch | No stable starting position | Tap forward while flat to find the sweet spot |

Corner jumps are the fine-tuning tool. Jumping off your corners can eke out a little extra height and distance, which matters on tight challenges. Community experience suggests using corner jumps as a supplement, not a replacement for clean sprint flips. If your foundation is messy, corner jumps will amplify the mistake.
